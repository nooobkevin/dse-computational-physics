# Quantum Physics Exercise — Concept Questions

These questions test your understanding of the physics behind the quantum
well simulation you just implemented.  Answer them in a few sentences each.

## Questions

### (a) Photon energy and the photoelectric effect

A metal surface has a work function of 2.3 eV.  Light of frequency
`f = 8.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz` is shone on the surface.

1. Calculate the energy of a single photon in eV.
2. Determine whether photoelectrons are emitted.  If so, find their
   maximum kinetic energy.
3. What is the stopping potential required to bring the most energetic
   photoelectrons to a stop?

*(Planck constant h = 4.136 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s, elementary charge e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)*

### (b) Wave-particle duality

Explain what is meant by **wave-particle duality** in quantum physics.
Give one piece of experimental evidence for:
- The wave nature of light
- The particle nature of light
- The wave nature of matter

### (c) de Broglie wavelength

A proton and an electron have the same kinetic energy.

1. Which particle has the longer de Broglie wavelength?  Explain your
   reasoning.
2. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at
   `v = 2.0 × 10⁶ m/s`.  (Electron mass `m_e = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg`,
   Planck constant `h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s`.)

### (d) Quantised energy levels in the infinite square well

An electron is confined to a one-dimensional infinite square well of
width `L = 0.5 nm`.

1. Calculate the ground-state energy `E₁` in eV.
2. Calculate `E₂` and `E₃`.
3. What is the wavelength of the photon emitted when the electron drops
   from `n = 2` to `n = 1`?
4. Explain why the energy levels get farther apart as `n` increases.

*(Planck constant h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, m_e = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg,
1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)*

### (e) Probability density and wavefunctions

The wavefunction for the ground state of the infinite square well is
`ψ₁(x) = √(2/L) sin(πx/L)`.

1. Sketch the shape of `ψ₁(x)` and `|ψ₁(x)|²` for `0 ≤ x ≤ L`.
2. Where is the particle most likely to be found?
3. What is the probability of finding the particle exactly at `x = L/2`?
   (Hint: consider the difference between probability and probability
   density.)
4. Show that `∫₀ᴸ |ψ₁(x)|² dx = 1`.

### (f) Heisenberg uncertainty principle

State the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.  Use the infinite square well
to illustrate it:

1. Estimate the minimum momentum uncertainty `Δp` for a particle confined
   to a well of width `L`.
2. How does the ground-state energy `E₁` relate to this uncertainty?
3. What would happen to `E₁` if the well width `L` were halved?
