## 1. Curriculum Spec

### 6. Physics and Society — Overview

Physics embedded in daily life; radioactivity and sustainable development as crucial concepts. Radioactive isotopes used in medicine and nuclear power; safety measures to minimise hazards. Nuclear power and renewable energy sources for combating climate change and achieving carbon neutrality. Physics-trained expertise applies modelling skills to financial analytics, social interactions, complex systems.

**Nature of Science:** Manhattan Project — cooperation between scientists and engineers; nuclear weapons in WWII; nuclear power as clean low-carbon energy source.

---

### 6a. Radiation and radioactivity

| Students should learn | Students should be able to |
|---|---|
| **X-rays** | • realise X-rays as ionising EM radiations of short wavelengths with high penetrating power<br>• realise emission of X-rays when fast electrons hit a heavy metal target<br>• discuss the medical and security use of X-rays |
| **α, β and γ radiations** | • describe the origin and nature of α, β and γ radiations<br>• compare α, β and γ in terms of penetrating power, ranges, ionising power, behaviour in electric field and magnetic field, and cloud chamber tracks |
| **Radioactive decay** | • realise occurrence of radioactive decay in unstable nuclides and its random nature<br>• state proportional relationship between activity of a sample and number of undecayed nuclei<br>• define half-life as period over which number of radioactive nuclei decreases by factor of one-half<br>• determine half-life from decay graph or numerical data |
| **Detection of radiation** | • realise existence of background radiation<br>• know methods in detecting radiations using photographic film and GM counter<br>• interpret radiation in terms of count rate using a GM counter |
| **Radiation safety** | • represent radiation equivalent dose using Sievert (Sv)<br>• discuss potential hazards of ionising radiation and suggest safety precautions in handling radioactive sources to minimise absorbed dose |
| **Radioactive isotopes** | • define atomic number (protons) and mass number (protons + neutrons)<br>• represent nuclides using symbolic notations<br>• define isotope<br>• realise existence of radioactive isotopes in some elements<br>• discuss uses of radioactive isotopes for radiotherapy, thickness inspection, tracers and medical imaging<br>• solve problems involving radioactivity and radioactive decay |

### 6b. Energy sources and sustainable development

| Students should learn | Students should be able to |
|---|---|
| **Renewable and low carbon energy sources** | • recognise needs and consideration for developing renewable and low carbon energy sources for achieving relevant SDGs<br>• realise release of energy in nuclear fission and fusion<br>• state mass-energy relationship ΔE = Δmc²<br>• use electron-volt (eV) and atomic mass unit as units of energy<br>• apply ΔE = Δmc² to determine energy release in nuclear reactions<br>• realise nuclear chain reaction |
| **Environmental impact of energy consumption** | • describe principle of nuclear fission reactor and state roles of moderator, coolant and control rods<br>• realise nuclear fusion as source of solar energy<br>• describe photovoltaic effect for a semiconductor to generate voltage and current upon exposure to light<br>• define solar constant as total EM radiation energy at normal incidence by Sun per unit time per unit area at mean Earth–Sun distance outside Earth's atmosphere<br>• solve problems involving solar constant<br>• derive maximum power by wind turbine as P = ½ηρAv³, solve problems<br>• discuss impact of extraction, conversion, distribution and greenhouse gas emission in use of energy on environment and society |
| **Carbon neutrality** | • discuss how daily habits of individuals contribute towards achieving carbon neutrality to mitigate climate change<br>• analyse consumption data for different energy sources in Hong Kong and their specific purposes<br>• appreciate nation's contribution to development of renewable and low carbon energy sources |

### Suggested Learning and Teaching Activities

- Measure background radiation using GM counter
- Demonstrate random variation of count rate using GM counter and radioactive source
- Identify sources of natural radiations; investigate why exposure is increased for airline crews and passengers
- Determine factors leading to increase in radon concentration in high-rise buildings
- Read specifications for commercial products containing radiation (e.g. smoke detectors)
- Assess risks and benefits of using nuclear radiations in medical diagnosis
- Search process of handling radioactive wastes in Hong Kong
- Estimate half-life from activity vs time graph
- Search for information on radioactive dating, radioactive tracers, food irradiation, product sterilisation
- Discuss worth and risks of using X-rays, ultrasound and radionuclides for patients
- Search and discuss ethics of developing and using nuclear weapons
- Examine efficiency of wind power generator with P = ½ηρAv³
- Examine photovoltaic effect of solar cell
- Study complex systems to simulate social or natural issues (crowd control, forest fire)
- Organise/participate in competition on solar cooker, wind power, solar car, hydrogen car race
- Study resonance effect from interaction between EM waves of particular wavelengths and greenhouse gases
- Study most recent scientific development of nuclear fission and fusion for nuclear power generation
- Visit low carbon energy source centre
- Visit local power plants and nuclear power plants in our country (e.g. Daya Bay)
- Participate talks by EMSD, Green Power, CLP, HK Electric, Towngas, MTR, EPD

### Suggested Computational Physics Activities

- Simulate radioactive decay process (line 2621)

### STSE Connections and Socio-Scientific Issues

- Search and analyse data of carbon emission and temperature change
- Analyse reasons causing serious nuclear disasters in history; study enhanced safety standards of new-generation nuclear power plants; limitations and advantages of small modular reactor (SMR)
- Debate comparing nuclear reactors with other methods of producing electrical power (cost-effectiveness, sustainable development, potential hazards)
- Investigate how enhancing technology improves safety and abundance of possible nuclear power fuels
- Study impact of extraction, conversion, distribution and use of energy on environment and society
- Discuss ways to save electrical energy for economy and environmental protection
- Discuss alternative generation and better use of electricity for more sustainable use in Hong Kong
- Discuss feasible plan for Hong Kong to achieve goals of sustainable development

## 4. Notes

### Ambiguities

- **Exponential law of decay (REMOVED per Annex 3, line 4296–4297):** The CAF removes the formal exponential decay equation A = A₀e^{-λt} and transmutation from the Radioactivity and Nuclear Energy content. However, the half-life definition (N decreases by factor of one-half) and determination from graph remain. The current unit uses N = N₀·2^{-t/T} (powers of two) rather than the natural exponential form — this is consistent with the removal. The `decay_probability` hook uses p = 1 - exp(-λ dt) internally, which is a computational implementation detail, not a curriculum outcome. **No change needed.**
- **Transmutation (REMOVED per Annex 3, line 4303):** The current unit does not cover transmutation. **No action needed.**
- **Medical imaging INTEGRATED-IN (Annex 3, lines 4258–4260):** The spec integrates "medical imaging applications of radioisotopes" from the former Medical Physics elective into this topic. This is item 6a.19 (uses of radioactive isotopes). The current unit has no coverage — this is a significant gap.
- **Energy content INTEGRATED-IN (Annex 3, lines 4255–4257):** The entire 6b sub-topic (renewable/non-renewable energy sources, environmental impact) was integrated from the former Energy and Use of Energy elective. This is the largest single gap in the current unit.

### Risks

- **Scope creep:** Adding all of 6b (energy sources, sustainable development, carbon neutrality) effectively doubles the unit's content. Consider whether this should remain a single unit or be split into two sub-units (06a Radioactivity, 06b Energy & Society).
- **physics_core expansion:** The energy domain requires new physics_core modules for solar constant, wind power, fission/fusion energy calculations. This is a significant engineering effort (Priority 1, item 1).
- **STSE activities not covered:** The suggested STSE connections (carbon emission data analysis, nuclear disaster case studies, SMR debate, HK energy plan) are discussion/debate activities that may not map well to the three-artifact (Manim + app + exercise) pattern. Consider adding a "discussion guide" document or teacher presentation slides.
- **Suggested L&T activities not covered:** Many suggested activities (GM counter measurements, radon investigation, smoke detector specs, radioactive waste handling, power plant visits, guest talks) are hands-on/experiential and outside the computational toolkit's scope. The README should note these as complementary teacher-led activities.

### Removed content still present in unit

- The current unit's README and scenes reference "Exponential decay law N = N₀·2^{-t/T}" — this is the half-life form, not the removed exponential law A = A₀e^{-λt}. The distinction is subtle but important. The README should be updated to use "half-life decay law" terminology to align with the CAF removal.
- The `decay.py` module internally computes λ = ln(2)/T and uses exp(-λ dt) — this is a computational implementation detail and does not need to change, but documentation should avoid calling it "exponential law of decay."
