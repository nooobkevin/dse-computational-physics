# Unit 05 Physics and Engineering — Curriculum Alignment (CAF Consultation Draft)

## 1. Curriculum spec

### Topic 5. Physics and Engineering (lines 2098–2354)

**Overview** (lines 2098–2120): Engineering applies scientific principles to solve problems across electricity generation, power transmission, and aerospace engineering. Motor/generator structure from electromagnetism and electromagnetic induction; AC stepped up/down via transformers for domestic use; household electricity safety; sensors for smart living; fluid dynamics, gravitational potential energy, and orbital motions for aerospace.

**Nature of Science** (lines 2124–2130): Edison (DC) vs Tesla (AC) — "War of the Currents" illustrating human element in science.

#### Suggested Learning and Teaching Activities (lines 2283–2327)
1. Investigate factors affecting magnitude of induced current/e.m.f. of a moving coil inside a magnetic field
2. Examine a transformer's voltage ratio and turns ratio
3. Investigate Faraday's law ε = −ΔΦ/Δt using signal generator across a solenoid and measure induced e.m.f. across a coil surrounding the solenoid using oscilloscope
4. Gather and analyse information on how high tension cables are insulated from power pylons, protected from lightning strikes and cleaned from dirt
5. Demonstrate conversion from mechanical energy to electrical energy using regenerative braking system
6. Demonstrate heating and damping effect from eddy current
7. Compare efficiency of cooking electrical appliances (microwave oven, electric heater, magnetic heater)
8. Compare power transmission among d.c. through cables and a.c. through stepping up and down of voltage in a grid system
9. Build a circuit to generate electricity by solar energy or wind turbine
10. Demonstrate Bernoulli's effect using water flow through pitot tube or horizontal tube with a narrow section
11. Demonstrate Coanda effect of curved surface under a fluid in motion (e.g. wind blow to circular cup, spoon under water stream)
12. Investigate shapes of air foil affecting the uplifting force under air stream
13. Investigate angle of attack against the uplifting force on air foil under air stream
14. Design and make an aircraft model using 3D printer
15. Search for information on home-developed aircraft C919
16. Compare working principles of helicopter, drone, hot air balloon and aircraft
17. Carry out an energy audit on own homes or schools
18. Gather and analyse use of sensors on own homes or schools and study how data transmitted and processed through computing and cloud systems for smart living
19. Construct models related to national aerospace technology (Long March rocket, Shenzhou spacecraft, Beidou satellite)
20. Read national space exploration projects (Tiangong, Lunar and Mars exploration)
21. Read story of Hong Kong's first astronaut entering Tiangong space station
22. Visit to museums/sites related to aerospace technology

#### Suggested Computational Physics activities (lines 2333–2335)
- Simulate the orbital motions of celestial bodies

#### STSE connections and socio-scientific issues (lines 2338–2349)
- Compare functions, cost and energy efficiency of LED with other light sources
- Study Energy Efficiency Labelling Scheme (EELS) in Hong Kong
- Gather and compare end-use energy efficiency for air conditioners, lamps and cars
- Study recent development of national aerospace technology and implication to society (low altitude economy, space economy)
- Discuss space competition and collaboration among different countries

## 4. Notes

### Ambiguities

1. **AC peak vs operating voltage**: Annex 3 deletes "root-mean-square value of alternating current" (line 4295), but the outcome "distinguish between the operating voltage/current and their peak values of an a.c. system" (line 2164–2166) remains. Teachers may still need to explain the √2 factor even without the formal RMS derivation. The toolkit should provide the numerical relationship without requiring RMS formula derivation.

2. **Transformer efficiency vs ideal model**: The CAF asks students to "examine methods for improving the efficiency of a transformer" (line 2176–2178), but the current implementation assumes an ideal transformer. A non-ideal model with core losses, copper losses, and eddy currents would be needed for quantitative efficiency discussion.

3. **Sensor scope**: The CAF says "recognise the basic working principle of common sensors that operate by detecting or measuring physical quantities and converting them to into electrical signals" (line 2214–2218). The phrase "common sensors" is broad — the toolkit should prioritise temperature (thermistor), light (LDR/photodiode), and motion (PIR) sensors as these are most accessible for classroom demonstration.

4. **Aerospace depth**: The CAF enriches "Aircraft in fluid" (lines 4385–4392) with continuity equation, Bernoulli's principle, Coanda effect, and pitot tube — but does NOT include the full Navier-Stokes or computational fluid dynamics. The toolkit should stay at the Bernoulli-equation level and avoid over-implementing.

5. **Orbital motion computational activity**: The only suggested computational physics activity is "simulate the orbital motions of celestial bodies" (line 2334). This is a clear mandate for a numerical integration simulation (Euler or Verlet) of orbital mechanics — the toolkit's `integrators.py` already provides these.

### Risks

1. **Scope mismatch**: The current unit is ~70% misaligned with the CAF. Three of six current sub-topics (TIR, lasers, semiconductors, particle accelerators) are not in the CAF Unit 05 at all. The CAF introduces three entirely new sub-topics (aerospace, domestic electricity, electromagnetic induction) with zero current coverage. This is the most misaligned unit in the toolkit.

2. **Engine dependency**: The aerospace and induction engines require new `physics_core` modules. The `src/physics_core/engineering/` package currently has only `optics.py`, `lasers.py`, and `motors.py`. Adding `orbital.py`, `aerospace.py`, and `induction.py` will triple the package size.

3. **Cross-unit coordination**: Moving lasers to Unit 07 (Quantum Physics) requires coordination — the laser engine, Manim scene, teacher app mode, and concept questions must be transferred, not just deleted.

4. **Student exercise complexity**: The current exercise has two simple hooks (critical_angle, total_internal_reflection). The new content (orbital mechanics, fluid dynamics, induction) involves more complex physics and longer code — the exercise design pattern may need adjustment for multi-hook exercises.

5. **Computational physics mandate**: The CAF explicitly requires orbital motion simulation as a computational physics activity. This is the strongest mandate in the entire CAF for a specific computational exercise — it must be the centrepiece of the rebuilt unit.
