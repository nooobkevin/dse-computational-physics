### Key Practical Tasks
- measure distance of an object using parallax method

### Suggested Learning and Teaching Activities
- construct a simple antenna and connect to SDR for receiving radio signals from different sources, such as remote microphone, aircrafts and celestial bodies
- study the evolution of stars and observe their positions from H-R diagram
- observe absorption line spectrum (e.g. Iodine vapor)
- observe spectra of celestial objects under telescope (e.g. H-alpha)
- demonstrate Doppler's effect using sound transmitter (e.g. rotating single-frequency sound emitter)
- perform experiment to examine objects' motions obey laws of classical physics in an inertial frame that moving in constant velocity
- perform Michelson Morley experiment to examine speed of light is constant and independent of the motion of light source or observer
- study the fast-travel muon from cosmos can be detected on the Earth surface before decay as evidence supporting special relativity
- use cloud chamber to observe cosmic rays from the Universe
- use optical device and tuned sound beats to observe something traveling to be length contracted and time dilated respectively to simulate the world observed under special relativity
- study Schwarzschild radius and the recent discoveries about black hole
- study the discovery of gravitational wave from the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory (LIGO) in 2016 that supported the prediction by Albert Einstein 100 years ago
- visit to the sites with astronomical instruments, such as Astropark and Ho Koon Nature Education cum Astronomical Centre
- observe astronomical phenomena with the naked eyes (colour/brightness of stars, constellations, meteor showers, lunar eclipse)
- use optical telescopes to observe (Moon surface, planetary events, sunspots, planets, comets, binary stars, nebula, deep sky objects, long-term observation projects)
- observe artificial satellites and receive radio signals from space stations using antenna
- appreciate the development of scientific research of our country by studying national large research facilities (FAST, LHAASO)

### Suggested Computational Physics Activities
1. Simulate Doppler effect
2. Simulate different frames of reference for simultaneity and time dilation

### STSE Connections
- study astrophysics discoveries which have stimulated the development of modern science and changed our ways of living
- study the impact of cosmic rays or celestial bodies from outer space and our reactions to them, such as meteor and solar wind
- compare the calendar dates among different cultures and relate them with astronomical observations

## 4. Notes

### Ambiguities
- **H-R diagram vs spectral classes**: The CAF removes "major spectral classes OBAFGKM" (Annex 3 line 4309) but retains "classify stars on the H-R diagram according to their luminosities and surface temperatures" (line 2998–2999). The H-R diagram inherently uses temperature (or spectral type) as one axis. The removal likely targets memorisation of the OBAFGKM sequence and temperature ranges, not the concept of stellar temperature classification itself. The toolkit's `SPECTRAL_CLASSES` table can remain as a pedagogical reference if marked non-examinable.
- **Doppler effect**: The CAF integrates the Doppler effect from the former Astronomy and Space Science elective (line 4248–4251). The current toolkit covers the relativistic Doppler formula for light. The curriculum spec (line 3012) gives `Δλ/λ₀ = vᵣ/c` — the classical (low-velocity) approximation. The toolkit's relativistic formula is a superset and is appropriate, but the exercise should also explicitly cover the `Δλ/λ₀ = vᵣ/c` form.
- **Parallax**: The curriculum lists parallax as both a learning outcome (line 2980) and a Key Practical Task (line 3078). A computational simulation is appropriate, but the spec envisions a *physical* measurement activity. The simulation should complement, not replace, hands-on parallax.

### Risks
- **Relativity is ENRICHED but entirely MISSING**: The CAF enriches relativity with frames of reference, invariant light speed, time dilation, length contraction, and spacetime diagram (lines 4397–4401). The current toolkit has only two conceptual questions on time dilation. This is the single largest gap — the second required Computational Physics activity ("simulate different frames of reference for simultaneity and time dilation", line 3134) has zero implementation. This is a P1 risk for CAF compliance.
- **H-R diagram / Stefan-Boltzmann law is a large block**: Items b.1–b.4 (blackbody curves, H-R diagram, Stefan-Boltzmann law, size estimation) form a coherent block that is entirely missing. This is the second-largest gap. The Stefan-Boltzmann law formula `L = 4πR²σT⁴` appears explicitly in the spec (line 3001–3003) and must be implemented.
- **Dark matter is a new inclusion**: The dark matter / rotation curve outcome (line 3017–3019) is present in the CAF but absent from the toolkit. It is a conceptually rich topic suitable for computational simulation.
- **Removed content still live**: The `SPECTRAL_CLASSES` table and its display in the teacher app and Manim scene correspond to content explicitly removed from the CAF. While useful as enrichment, this could confuse teachers about what is examinable.
