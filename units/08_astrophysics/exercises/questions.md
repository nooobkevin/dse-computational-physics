# Astrophysics & Relativity Exercise — Concept Questions

These questions test your understanding of the physics behind the Doppler
shift and Hubble's law simulations you just implemented.  Answer them in a
few sentences each.

---

## Questions

### (a) Doppler redshift — meaning

You observe light from a distant galaxy and measure that its hydrogen lines
are shifted toward the red end of the spectrum.

1. What does a redshift tell you about the motion of the galaxy relative to
   Earth?
2. If the galaxy were approaching Earth instead, would you expect the same
   lines to shift toward red or toward blue?  Why?
3. In one sentence, explain why an approaching source produces a *higher*
   observed frequency.

### (b) Redshift z = Δλ/λ

A galaxy has a measured redshift of `z = 0.05`.

1. Using the low-velocity approximation `z ≈ v/c`, estimate the recession
   velocity of the galaxy in km/s.  (Take `c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s`.)
2. A second galaxy has `z = 0.10`.  How much faster (as a ratio) is it
   receding than the first galaxy?
3. The relativistic formula `z = sqrt((1+β)/(1-β)) − 1` gives a slightly
   different answer than `z ≈ v/c`.  State in one sentence why the two
   only agree when `v` is much smaller than `c`.

### (c) Hubble's law and the expanding universe

Two galaxies are at distances of `100 Mpc` and `400 Mpc` from Earth.

1. Using `H₀ = 67.8 km/s/Mpc`, compute the recession velocity of each
   galaxy.
2. What is the ratio of the two recession velocities?  How does this ratio
   relate to the ratio of the distances?
3. Explain why the *ratio* of velocities being the same as the ratio of
   distances (rather than both galaxies receding at the same speed) is
   evidence for an expanding universe rather than Earth being specially
   located at the centre of the universe.

### (d) Evidence for the Big Bang

List three pieces of observational evidence that support the Big Bang
theory, and state in one sentence each what each piece shows.

### (e) Stellar life cycle

A star with a mass of about `1 M☉` (like the Sun) follows a sequence of
stages.  A star with `20 M☉` eventually meets a very different fate.

1. List the main stages of the Sun's life cycle, starting from formation.
2. What are the two possible end-states of a high-mass star?
3. What physical factor — mass or luminosity — determines the eventual
   fate of a star?  Give the approximate mass threshold.

### (f) Time dilation (special relativity)

An astronaut travels at a speed close to the speed of light and returns to
Earth after what she measures as 1 year of travel.  Her twin brother on
Earth has aged more than 1 year.

1. State the name of this effect.
2. In one or two sentences, explain the physical reason the twins age
   differently, and which twin is younger on the return.
3. Does time dilation only apply to moving clocks, or does it also apply
   to moving biological processes such as ageing?  Explain briefly.
